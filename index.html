Your day

Starts Wednesday, Oct 21. From then on, today opens by itself. Every day: 3 new words, 3 expressions and 3 or 4 short tasks (about 40 minutes). Missed a day? No problem. Just do today's day.

The 4-step answer: works for any question

  1. AnswerSay your answer directly. "In my opinion, AI is very useful."
  2. ReasonSay why. "…because it saves time and helps people learn."
  3. ExampleGive one real example. "For example, I use it to practice my English."
  4. CloseFinish clearly. "So overall, I think AI is a great tool if we use it wisely."

These four parts take about one minute. Never stop in the middle; finish with the Close line.

Confidence

Speaking withcalm confidence

You understand English well. What stops you is the moment of freezing when you must talk alone. That freeze is not a language problem; it's a stress reaction, and it can be trained away like a muscle.

Why you freeze

  • Stress blocks memory. When you're nervous, your brain protects you and the words you know “disappear”.
  • Translating word by word. You build the sentence in Arabic first, then translate it. That's slow, so you stop.
  • Fear of mistakes. You check every word for grammar before you say it, so nothing comes out.

The truth about the test

  • Examiners score clear communication and fluency more than perfect grammar.
  • Small mistakes are normal. Native speakers make them and say “um” too.
  • A simple, complete answer beats a perfect half answer.
  • You already passed the harder test, the interpretation one.

Before you speak (2 minutes)

  1. Box breathingBreathe in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. Do it 3 times. It calms your body in one minute.
  2. Power poseStand tall, shoulders back, hands on hips, for 2 minutes before practice or a test (from Amy Cuddy's talk below).
  3. SmileSmiling relaxes your voice. People can hear a smile even on the phone.
  4. Know your first sentenceHave a ready opening: “That's a great question. Well, to be honest…” The first sentence starts the engine.

While you speak

Do

  • Slow down. Calm and slow sounds confident.
  • Use short, simple sentences.
  • Pause silently instead of saying “umm”.
  • Follow the 4 steps: answer, reason, example, close.
  • Look at the camera, like it's a friendly person.
  • Keep your hands relaxed and visible.

Rescue phrases

  • Mind goes blank: “Let me think about that for a second.”
  • Forgot a word: “It's a kind of…” / “It's something you use to…”
  • Made a mistake: “Sorry, I mean…” then continue.
  • Lost the point: “What I'm trying to say is…”
  • Need to finish: “So overall, that's why I think…”
  • Live interview, didn't understand: “Sorry, could you repeat that, please?”

Build it step by step

Confidence grows when you go one small step past your comfort zone, again and again. Move up a step when the current one feels easy.

  1. Talk to yourselfDescribe what you're doing, out loud, in English, at home.
  2. Voice notesRecord yourself and listen. Nobody else hears it.
  3. Claude voice modeA patient partner who never judges you.
  4. CameraRecord answers on video, like the real test.
  5. A real personA friend, Nour, or a partner on Tandem / HelloTalk.
  6. Mock interviewA tutor or Claude playing a strict interviewer.

The 12 rules that pass the test

These come from the US national standards (NCIHC Code of Ethics and Standards of Practice), which every American healthcare interpreting company follows. Raters listen for them.

  1. Interpret everything.No adding, no leaving out, no summarizing. Repeat it even if it's rude, repeated, or seems unimportant.
  2. Always first person.Doctor: "Do you have a fever?" You: "هل عندك حرارة؟" Never "He's asking if…". Patient: "My chest hurts", never "She says her chest hurts".
  3. Keep the tone and level.Formal stays formal, angry stays angry, medical words stay medical. Don't simplify the doctor's words yourself. If the patient doesn't understand, they ask the doctor.
  4. When you speak for yourself, say "the interpreter"."The interpreter needs a repetition." Then tell the other side what you just asked. This is called transparency.
  5. Never give advice or your opinion.If the patient asks you "What should I do?", you interpret the question to the doctor. You don't answer it.
  6. Never lose numbers, negatives, or time.Doses, dates, "not", "only", "for three days". Write numbers down the moment you hear them.
  7. Don't convert or explain.Don't turn ounces into cups or Celsius into Fahrenheit. Converting is adding.
  8. Correct yourself right away."Interpreter correction: the doctor said fifty milligrams, not fifteen."
  9. Manage the flow politely.If someone talks too long: "Excuse me, the interpreter needs to interpret now." Then continue.
  10. Confidentiality (HIPAA).Nothing from a call leaves the call. Destroy notes after every call. Never record.
  11. Stay neutral.No side-chats with the patient. If you know the patient personally, tell the company and step out.
  12. Safety first.Anything about suicide, abuse, chest pain, or allergy is interpreted immediately and completely, even if the patient says "don't tell the doctor".

The NCIHC Code of Ethics, 9 principles (memorize the names)

#PrincipleIn one line
1ConfidentialityEverything stays inside the care team.
2AccuracyFull message and its spirit, nothing added or omitted.
3ImpartialityNo opinions, no bias, no advice.
4RespectTreat every party with dignity.
5Cultural awarenessKnow both cultures, including US hospital culture.

Least-intrusive rule: first just interpret. If needed, clarify a word. If needed, flag a possible cultural misunderstanding. Advocacy is last.

The traps

  • 15 vs 50, 13 vs 30, 14 vs 40. On the phone they sound alike. If not 100% sure, ask: "The interpreter requests a repetition of the number."
  • Always write the unit with the number: 500 mg, 10 units, 2 puffs.
  • Frequency + duration: "twice a day for 10 days." Don't drop the second half.
  • Don't convert Celsius↔Fahrenheit, kg↔lb, ounces↔cups.
  • Read blood pressure as "148 over 95" / ١٤٨ على ٩٥.
  • Money: "$4.15" = four dollars and fifteen cents / أربعة دولارات وخمسة عشر سنتًا.

Getting paid in Egypt

  • PayPal: link an Egyptian Visa card to receive and withdraw money.

  • Wise: used by Multilingual.
  • Bank account in your name: needed for Mercor (Stripe).

Scam warning signs

  • Any fee: registration, training, “starter kit”, certificate.
  • A “recruiter” who moves you to Telegram or WhatsApp only.
  • Hired in minutes with no test or interview.
  • Asking for your ID, card or bank login in chat.
  • Emails not from the company's own website domain.
